Nagamese is the lingua franca of Nagaland, yet it has almost no NLP resources: no large corpora, no standard benchmarks and no language models. NagaNLP is my attempt to change that. It is a paper on arXiv ([2512.12537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12537)), and its datasets and models are released on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/collections/agnivamaiti/naganlp-project).

**Paper:** *NagaNLP: Bootstrapping NLP for Low-Resource Nagamese Creole with Human-in-the-Loop Synthetic Data.* Agniva Maiti, Manya Pandey, Murari Mandal.

## Method

1. An LLM (Gemini), guided by expert prompts, generates a candidate Nagamese corpus.
2. Native speakers correct and annotate it. Inter-annotator agreement (Cohen's κ) is 0.92 for POS and 0.88 for NER.
3. The result is a **10,018-pair conversational dataset** plus POS-tagged and NER-annotated corpora.

## Results (as reported in the paper)

| Task | Model | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Part-of-speech tagging | XLM-RoBERTa-base, fine-tuned | 93.81% accuracy (0.90 macro-F1) |
| Named entity recognition | XLM-RoBERTa-base, fine-tuned | 0.75 macro-F1 |
| Conversation | NagaLLaMA (Llama-3.2-3B Instruct, LoRA) | perplexity 3.85 vs 96.76 few-shot |

## Stack

Python, PyTorch, Hugging Face Transformers, LoRA fine-tuning, human annotation guidelines and agreement analysis.

## Why it matters

The pipeline is meant to be reusable for any language that has speakers but no corpora: a model drafts the data and people check it. The same work powers [NagaTranslate](/projects/NagaTranslate).
